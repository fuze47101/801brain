Key Insight: The Pump is the Problem

Your dad's well pump is almost certainly a 3–7.5 HP submersible pulling 15–40+ amps at 240V. Running continuously during summer irrigation, that's 3,600–9,000+ kWh/month from the pump alone. The tiered rate structure means all that pump usage hits the expensive Block 2 rate at $0.1137/kWh. Every kWh we offset with solar avoids this top-tier rate — making the ROI better than the blended average suggests.

3.3 Net Metering / Export Situation

Rocky Mountain Power Net Metering — Important Context

The traditional net metering program (Schedule 135) is closed to new customers. New solar installations fall under Schedule 137 (Net Billing), which pays an export credit rate of only ~$0.056/kWh in summer and ~$0.047/kWh in winter — roughly half the retail rate. This means self-consumption and battery storage are far more valuable than exporting to the grid. Every kWh you store and use yourself is worth $0.10–$0.11 avoided cost, vs. $0.05 if exported. This dramatically favors a battery-backed system design.

Why Off-Grid + Diesel Beats Grid-Tied Here

Three factors converge to make Mode A the right call for Roger: (1) RMP Schedule 137 net billing pays only ~$0.04/kWh for exports vs. ~$0.12/kWh retail for imports, so the export credit never justifies the cert and paperwork burden; (2) Roger already owns a diesel generator that solves the multi-day cloudy-winter problem the grid connection would otherwise solve; and (3) off-grid operation unlocks Sunwave (CSA-listed) as a viable inverter — saving roughly $9,000 landed versus Japower all-in-one and ~$28,000 versus the Japower 60 kW option. 5.4 Battery Chemistry — LiFePO₄ (LFP)

About the LiFePO₄ Inside the Japower Units

The Japower All-in-One ESS uses LiFePO₄ (Lithium Iron Phosphate) cells — the safest lithium battery chemistry available. No thermal runaway risk, no cobalt, and a cycle life of 6,000+ cycles at 80% depth of discharge. That translates to 16+ years of daily cycling before reaching 80% original capacity. Round-trip efficiency is 95–97%, meaning virtually no energy is lost in the charge/discharge cycle. The integrated BMS handles cell balancing, over/under voltage protection, temperature monitoring, and short circuit protection — all factory-calibrated and tested before shipping.

The Real Math on Rate Escalation

Rocky Mountain Power residential rates have increased an average of 3.5–5% per year over the last decade. At 4% annual escalation, your dad's current ~$3,500/year electric bill becomes ~$5,800/year in 10 years and ~$9,600/year in 25 years. 8. Installation Approach

8.1 Recommended DIY + Licensed Electrician Approach

Given your family's hands-on capability, significant cost can be saved by self-performing the mechanical work and hiring a licensed electrician only for the grid interconnection, panel hookup, and inspection-required work.

Phase 1: Site Prep & Foundations (DIY — 2-3 weekends)

Survey and mark tracker post locations along south and west fenceline. Dig or auger concrete pier holes (12"–16" diameter, 48" deep — below Tooele County frost line). Pour concrete with J-bolts or embedded post brackets. Allow 7 days cure time.

Phase 2: Trenching & Conduit (DIY or rent trencher — 1-2 days)

Trench from array runs to power center location. Lay 2" Schedule 40 PVC conduit with pull rope. Trench depth: 24" minimum. Pull DC home-run wires after tracker installation.

Phase 3: Tracker Assembly & Panel Mount (DIY — 2-3 weekends)

Assemble tracker frames on posts. Mount panels. Connect tracker actuators and controllers. Test tracking movement. Wire panels in series strings (typically 2 panels per tracker in series).

Phase 4: Japower ESS Installation (Licensed Electrician — 1 day)

Place Japower All-in-One ESS unit(s) on concrete pad near main panel. Connect DC combiner boxes to ESS solar input. Wire AC output to main panel. For Tier 3, parallel the two units per manufacturer instructions. No battery rack assembly, no ATS wiring, no battery shed — the all-in-one design cuts this phase from 2 days to 1.

Phase 5: Inspection & Interconnection (1-2 weeks)

Schedule Tooele County electrical inspection. Submit Rocky Mountain Power interconnection application (Schedule 137). Install utility-required AC disconnect at meter. Receive Permission to Operate (PTO).

Phase 6: Commissioning & Monitoring (1 day)

Power up system. Configure Japower ESS settings (self-consumption priority, battery charge/discharge limits, export limits). Set up app-based monitoring. Verify tracker alignment and calibration. Test backup/island mode.

10. Important Considerations

US Tariffs on Chinese Solar Equipment (2025–2026)

As of 2025–2026, US tariffs on Chinese solar panels and cells range from 25–50% depending on product classification (Section 201, Section 301, AD/CVD duties). However, several strategies can mitigate this:

1. Southeast Asian assembly: Many Tier-1 Chinese manufacturers (LONGi, Trina, JA) have assembly plants in Vietnam, Cambodia, Thailand, and Malaysia. Panels assembled there may face lower or no AD/CVD duties (though this landscape changes — verify current rates at time of order).

2. Trackers and batteries generally face lower tariff rates than panels/cells (verify HTS codes at time of import).

3. Even with tariffs, direct-sourced pricing is often still 30–50% below US retail distributor pricing.

4. Consult a customs broker before placing orders to confirm current duty rates for your specific HTS classifications.

Federal ITC — REPEALED for Residential Owners

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, terminated the 30% residential solar tax credit (Section 25D) on December 31, 2025. No step-down, no transition period. For a residential system placed in service in 2026, the owner receives $0 in federal tax credit. This removes approximately $17,000 of assumed savings from the Tier 3 case.

What's still available (not assumed in this document's economics):

1. Section 48E commercial ITC (30%) is still alive for projects that begin construction by July 4, 2026 and are placed in service by end of 2027. If the property is structured as a farm, ranch, or LLC business operation, the system may qualify under 48E instead of 25D. This is a time-sensitive window — verify with a CPA before April/May to preserve eligibility.

2. Third-party ownership (lease/PPA) — the lessor can still claim 48E through 2027 and pass some savings through. Generally worse economics for an owner with capital and direct-sourcing leverage.

Bottom line: The economics throughout this document reflect the residential-owner / no-ITC case. If an ag/business structure is viable, restore the 30% credit and subtract it from each tier's net cost.

Utah State Incentives

Utah's state renewable energy tax credit has been phased out, but the property tax exemption for renewable energy systems is still in effect — the added value of the solar system is exempt from property tax assessment for the first 8 years. This matters on a system worth $45K–$65K. Additionally, check with Tooele County for any local incentives or rebates that may apply.

11. Recommendation

Given the property size, available fenceline, your China sourcing capability, Roger's existing diesel generator, and his prepper sensibility — I'd recommend going straight to Tier 3 in a fully off-grid Mode A architecture with Sunwave as the ESS path, pending three verifications (Sunwave off-grid capability, Tooele County permit acceptance of CSA listing, and a CPA call on 48E ag eligibility). Here's why:

Dropping the grid connection is the move that unlocks everything else. RMP's Schedule 137 net billing is structurally unattractive — ~$0.04/kWh export credit is never going to justify the UL 1741 SA inverter constraint. Once you remove that constraint, Sunwave becomes viable, which is the cheapest $/kWh ESS on the table by a wide margin. Roger's existing diesel genny removes the last remaining reason to keep the grid (multi-day winter backup), and the diesel wires into the Sunwave inverter's AC input as a drop-in extension of the control logic. 160 kWh of LFP storage plus unlimited diesel runtime is materially more resilient than any grid-tied system would be.

6. Engage Licensed Electrician

Find a Tooele County electrician experienced with solar PV interconnection. They'll pull the permit, connect the Japower ESS to the main panel, and be present for inspection. The all-in-one design simplifies their scope significantly. Estimated labor: $1,500–$3,000 depending on tier.
