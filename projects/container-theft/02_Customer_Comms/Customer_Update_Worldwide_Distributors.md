## What happened

The container arrived at the Port of Seattle on March 16, 2026. It should have been released to a delivery carrier within days. Instead, it sat at the SSA Marine terminal for over a month because of failures by the China-side shipping arranger and its US agents — failures that had nothing to do with you or with my company.

Specifically, and now documented in writing:

1. **The shipment was filed with US Customs without proper authorization from me as the importer.** US Customs placed an enforcement hold on February 11. The hold was caused by missing documentation and an unauthorized power of attorney that the shipping agents filed without my knowledge.

2. **The consignee was changed from my company to an unaffiliated third party** ("Great Way Trading & Transportation") — without my consent.

3. **The cargo was misdeclared to US Customs as "fishing line" instead of sleeping bags**, with an incorrect tariff classification.

4. **A valid power of attorney from my company was not provided to the licensed customs broker until March 29, 2026.** US Customs cleared the container approximately one week later, on April 7.

5. The container then sat at the terminal for two more weeks because the trucking party with control failed to retrieve it. I have written confirmation from SSA Marine that the container never left the terminal during this period.

6. On April 21, the agents finally moved the container — and immediately invoiced me approximately **$34,000** in "demurrage and inspection" charges. Their own internal records show their actual cost was lower, and SSA Marine has confirmed in writing that no inspection of the container ever took place. I have refused to pay the inflated amount.

The shipment was sold to me on **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) terms**. Under DDP, I had already paid for all-in delivery to the destination as part of the original purchase. I owe nothing further for shipping, customs, or delivery — and I have wire records to prove what was paid.
